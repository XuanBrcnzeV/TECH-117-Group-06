Traffic Light Warning System

An intelligent real-time driver alert system designed to prevent red-light running, reduce intersection collisions, and improve road safety in low-visibility conditions.

1. Introduction

Intersections are among the most high-risk areas on modern roadways due to sudden signal transitions, driver distraction, and adverse weather conditions such as fog or heavy rain. Our Traffic Light Warning System addresses this safety gap by detecting upcoming traffic signal states and delivering timely visual and auditory warnings inside the vehicle, giving drivers sufficient reaction time to decelerate safely.

🎯 Project Goals & Success Criteria

1. Project Goals (What the project is intended to do):

  • Real-Time Signal Detection: Accurately identify traffic light states (Green, Yellow, Red) prior to the vehicle reaching the intersection stop line.
  • Timely Driver Alerts: Deliver clear visual dashboard indicators and audio chimes to warn drivers of an impending Yellow or Red light.
  • Safety Enhancement: Reduce late braking and unintentional red-light violations during distracted driving or poor visibility.

2. Success Criteria (How success will be measured):

  • Detection Accuracy: The prototype correctly classifies Green, Yellow, and Red signals with at least 95% accuracy across 50 test runs.
  • Alert Latency: System response time from signal change to driver warning display is less than 0.5 seconds.
  • User Comprehension: At least 85% of test participants correctly interpret the warning level without confusion during usability testing.

⚠️ Constraints & Risks

1. Project Constraints (Limitations):

  • Time: The one-semester timeline limits testing to controlled scale models or simulated environments rather than live public roads.
  • Budget: A strict student project budget prevents purchasing expensive commercial V2X (Vehicle-to-Everything) transceivers.
  • Resources & Skills: Limited access to outdoor testing tracks and varying levels of embedded programming experience within the team.

2. Risks & Backup Plans:

  • Risk 1 — Lighting & Glare Interference: Ambient sunlight or reflections may cause false color readings.
    Backup Plan: Apply HSV color-space filtering in software and conduct primary demonstrations under controlled indoor lighting.
  • Risk 2 — Hardware Failure or Shipping Delays: Microcontroller or sensor components may arrive late or malfunction.
    Backup Plan: Maintain a fully interactive web-based software simulation (shown below) to demonstrate system logic and alert timing on schedule.
  • Risk 3 — Scope Creep: Adding too many features (e.g., pedestrian tracking) may delay core deliverables.
    Backup Plan: Prioritize core signal-warning functionality first and treat extra features as optional stretch goals.

3. Research & References

The following research articles, safety reports, and real-world implementations support the technical design and necessity of our Traffic Light Warning System:

  • U.S. Department of Transportation (USDOT) – Connected Vehicle Applications (Red Light Violation Warning):
    Explains how Vehicle-to-Infrastructure (V2I) communication warns drivers when they are predicted to enter a signalized intersection against a red light.
    https://www.its.dot.gov/cv_basics/cv_basics_what.htm
  • Audi Traffic Light Information (TLI) System – Industry Inspiration:
    A real-world commercial deployment demonstrating how in-dash traffic signal alerts and speed advisories improve traffic flow and reduce sudden braking.
    https://media.audiusa.com/releases/324
  • National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) – Intersection Safety Research:
    Provides statistical evidence on red-light running fatalities and how early driver warning systems reduce collision severity.
    https://www.nhtsa.gov/road-safety/intersection-safety
  • IEEE Xplore – Real-Time Traffic Light Recognition for Driver Assistance Systems:
    Technical study detailing color segmentation, bounding-box detection, and low-latency alert algorithms used in modern driver-assistance prototypes.
    https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8917165
